Exploratory Data Analysis

Daily data for 2019-01-01 to 2023-12-31 (1826 days per city, no missing dates, values or duplicates) from the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API. Select a figure to enlarge it.

1. Distributions

Smoothed distributions (kernel density) of daily max temperature, mean humidity, max wind speed and rain (rainy days only). Delhi has by far the widest temperature range.

2. Seasonal temperature pattern

Max temperature by IMD season (Winter Dec–Feb, Pre-monsoon Mar–May, Monsoon Jun–Sep, Post-monsoon Oct–Nov). Delhi varies strongly between seasons; coastal Mumbai varies much less.

3. Same-day correlations

Pearson correlations between same-day variables. Humidity is clearly negatively correlated with max temperature in all three cities (−0.60 to −0.77). Rain is negatively correlated in Bengaluru (−0.32) and Mumbai (−0.41) but not in Delhi (−0.03). Correlation does not imply causation.

4. Raw autocorrelation

Correlation of max temperature with itself k days earlier. It stays high for weeks (e.g. Delhi 0.89 at lag 7, 0.70 at lag 30) mainly because of the seasonal cycle, so on its own it does not determine the lag window. See figure 6.

5. City comparison

Mean max temperature, range (highest − lowest daily max) and average number of days per year with max ≥ 35 °C.

6. Day-to-day memory (lag window)

Left: autocorrelation after removing the day-of-year average; it falls to about 0.24–0.38 by lag 7. Right: partial autocorrelation, dominated by lag 1 with small values up to about a week. A 7-day window is a reasonable choice, not a sharp cutoff.

7. Ensemble residuals on the 2023 test year

Residual = actual − ensemble prediction for the selected city, with the 10th/90th-percentile offsets (orange) that define the 80% interval.
